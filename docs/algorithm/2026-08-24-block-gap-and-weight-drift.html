Five green rows, one amber, and a headline delta of 0.24 that reads as "basically even". But the amber row is 4.5x worse than the next-worst skill, and the composite delta hides it entirely: the composite is a weighted sum, so a big block gap in one direction gets cancelled by small gaps in the other five. The one number the group anchors on is the one number that structurally cannot show this problem.

Root cause 1 — the objective rewards hiding imbalance in one skill real bug

_optimal_assign in src/toop/balance.py searches every possible split and minimises:

Σskill weight · |gapskill|

A sum of absolute gaps has no opinion about how the imbalance is distributed. A split with six 0.23 gaps and a split with one 0.73 gap plus five near-zeros score almost identically, and the optimiser will pick whichever is a hair lower. On Monday it picked the spike: it shaved attack to 0.03 and serve to 0.10 by handing Team A both of the group's top-3 blockers.

It was fixable. Swapping Ali Sh ↔ Morteza drops the block gap from 0.73 to 0.20. The cost is receive going to 0.43 and serve to 0.54, and the total across all skills rising from 1.38 to 1.77. The current objective refuses that trade because it only reads the total. A human captain would take it in a second.

This is not a one-off

The fix is one character. Change abs(...) to a square in _optimal_assign. Squaring makes a 0.73 gap cost 4.5x what four 0.2 gaps cost, so the optimiser stops treating one lopsided skill as free. No new thresholds to tune, no extra config, and the exhaustive search is unchanged.

But attack is currently weighted 2.4x block in the objective, which is the precise bias that makes the optimiser willing to trade away block, and it also inflates every composite total the group sees. It is a live landmine even though it was not this week's trigger.

Root cause 3 — "confidence: low" is permanent, and it is quietly true real

CALIBRATION_THRESHOLD=15, but the most-rated player in the whole group has 11 ratings per skill. Nobody has ever been calibrated. Every snapshot since session 3 has printed confidence: low, so the group has been reading a fairness disclaimer on every single message and has learned to ignore it.

Root cause 4 — thin-data players get flattened toward "average" real

refresh_ratings shrinks each estimate by dividing by (votes + SHRINKAGE_K) with K=3. That is the right idea, but with this vote spread it bites unevenly:

Everyone the group has the strongest gut feelings about (the rare attendees, the new faces) is pulled toward the middle, while the regulars keep their edge. Homayoun's six skills are all within 0.21 of each other on two ratings, which is not a player profile, it is a placeholder. Turning shrinkage off moves 4 of the 14 players to the other team, so the split is genuinely sensitive to a knob nobody has validated against how the games actually go.

And a smaller one hiding underneath it

NORM_MIN_RATINGS=8 gates rater z-scoring on score count, not on how many different people the rater scored. Two raters clear the gate on a sample far too narrow to z-score against:

  • Morteza — 14 scores across only 3 players
  • EM — 27 scores across only 5 players

Z-scoring forces those handfuls of players to spread across the full range with mean 0 and sd 1, whether or not they are actually different from each other. It injects noise into exactly the low-coverage players who are already the shakiest.

So: is the algorithm broken, or is it a communication problem?

Both, and in that order. There is a real defect (root cause 1) that produced a genuinely lopsided net on Monday and has done so in roughly half of all sessions. Fixing the message without fixing that would be gaslighting the group about something they correctly noticed.

But the presentation is also actively working against you:

  • The headline number cannot show the problem. Δ 0.24 is a weighted sum, so opposite-sign skill gaps cancel. A team can be 0.73 ahead at the net and still post a tiny delta. Lead with the worst skill gap, not the composite.
  • The amber threshold is too generous. _FAIR_OK = 0.80 meant a 0.73 gap rendered as 🟨 "قابل‌قبول". Given that everything else lands under 0.30, anything past ~0.45 should be red.
  • The units are meaningless to a volleyball group. "0.73" is a sum of shrunk z-scores. Nobody can sanity-check it, so they fall back to eyeballing the roster, which is where the mistrust comes from.
  • The bars are decorative, not evidential. Five greens and an amber say "trust me". Naming the specific trade the bot made ("Team A is stronger at the net; Team B is stronger on serve") is a claim they can check against what they felt.

On adding a chart for the group

Do not send them this page. A Telegram group does not want a dashboard, and a richer visual would make the current numbers more convincing rather than more correct. Items 3, 4 and 5 are the transparency fix: keep the emoji bars (they render fine on every phone and survive RTL), make the colours honest, and add one plain sentence naming the trade-off. That is the whole intervention.

The one thing genuinely worth adding once the objective is fixed: after the teams are published, a line like «۳۴۳۲ ترکیب بررسی شد، این متعادل‌ترینش بود». It is true (the search really is exhaustive over all C(14,7) splits), it is verifiable, and it answers the actual accusation, which is not "the numbers are off by 0.1" but "the bot is not really trying".
